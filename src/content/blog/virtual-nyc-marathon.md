While chatting with people about the planning and how this all came about, I kept saying, "This is so dumb." 

I feel like the draw to run marathons (and specifically all the majors) has really gone down for me since the [Berlin Marathon in 2024](https://www.strava.com/activities/12530500399). I got a PB I was really happy with and had an amazing race, better than I could've hoped for. I really felt content after that, but somehow I still find myself (of my own free will) getting roped into endurance things. 

This all originally came from the idea with my friend Tanner to try to circumvent the NYC marathon lottery (again, I don't really want to do another marathon, but the challenge of just getting in was pretty appealing). 

We tried to qualify by running the [Brooklyn RBC Half Marathon](https://www.strava.com/activities/18528705166/overview) back in May, but fell short by about 2 minutes. By around 5k we knew the 1:21 qualifying time wasn't going to happen. I feel like my training was just kind of "meh" from earlier in the year all the way through the spring, which is why [Boston](https://www.strava.com/activities/18186603028) didn't go exactly to plan. Either way still a fun time, but mission failed. 

Then Tanner found out about the virtual NYC marathon, and how if you complete it, then you get a guaranteed entry for the following year. This became our goal, but we weren't even able to get into the virtual marathon lottery. 

Then Tanner found out about the CHARITY virtual NYC marathon, and how if you complete that AND raise $2,000 for NYRR, then you get a guaranteed entry to the NYC marathon for the following year. 

It seemed like a multi-level marketing scam, but we still applied and of course got in. 

## Virtual Race Day Prep

We planned to run the marathon together in LA, the weekend of Halloween. With a chaotic fall schedule and new job, plans didn't align so we ended up having to run them on opposite coasts. I was in NYC that week for work, so decided to run the virtual NYC marathon, in NYC, during the same weekend of the actual NYC marathon. 

For some reason, I had done a few of the longer training runs on Friday morning before work so it seemed like the best idea was to also just run the full marathon Friday morning before work to be able to fully enjoy the weekend in NYC. 

I looked at a few different routes, but settled on lapping the West Side Highway to prevent any stops from crossings, cars, etc. 

## Virtual Race Day

I woke up at 4:30am, ran a few warm-up blocks from a hotel in the Upper West Side over to the path, and at 5:15 just headed south. 

There was a moderate headwind coming out of the south that felt totally fine on the first lap. I went all the way down to Battery Park City, flipped around and headed back north towards the GW Bridge. Some guy commuting to work with a backpack latched on to my pace (6:40ish) and stayed with me for about 4 mi.  

I made it up north, got a bottle handoff from Jenn on an E-bike since there were no fountains between mile 8-16. 

Once I flipped around, the headwind piled onto my tired legs and miles 16-20 felt like hell to keep pace. I thought several times I was going off the cliff, but managed to hold pretty steady up until mile 23. 

That's where I started fading. I never ran more than 45–50 miles a week in training, so fatigue there made sense. I drifted into the 6:50s but luckily never went over 7:00. 

I unceremoniously crossed the finish line up by 134th St, and walked 1.5 miles to the Citi Bike rack, and then biked 3 mi back south on the path to the hotel. 

I took a quick shower where my legs felt like Jello, and then hopped on the 2 train to go to my office downtown. I worked there until 7pm fueled on my 3 caffeine gels from the morning and then had a nice double date with a new coworker and his wife close to our hotel. 

## Takeaways

Looking back, it's weird how much a race day just felt like a typical day. 

I ran [one of the best runs I ever have](https://www.strava.com/activities/20790185586), basically running the same pace as the [first time I broke sub-3 hours for a marathon](https://www.strava.com/activities/6748351467), which at the time (and how I wrote about it) felt like a major life achievement. 

It feels odd?/good? that as time and life go by, the original goal and challenges that seemed so hard all shift. Something that once was a fixation that took multiple years to pull off became something I could handle before a full workday. 

The whole thing objectively was idiotic, but it was a bit of a different learning experience and challenge that I'm happy to have put myself through. A lot of all the endurance stuff is just finding the line for what you can handle. 

I ended up getting 3rd out of 1,000+ people that did the virtual marathon which I'm proud of. I was in first place until the last evening of the race window. 

I guess the good news is I get to run another marathon next year.
